My projects

Brand social channels built from scratch

01

My first task at the company was to build external communication with candidates. All that existed was a VK group of 250 people.

I started with research: how competitors speak, to whom, through which channels. Fashion retail had almost no career channels — hiring ran entirely through job boards. I decided to move away from that and reach young candidates through content made by their own peers.

I built the concept and the formats, wrote the idea for every clip, shot and edited most of it myself, and delegated the rest to employees in stores across the country. Growth came with no paid promotion: giveaways, cross-posts from the brands' product channels, partnerships with universities and shopping malls.

A community of 30 creators

02

The channels grew and one person could no longer feed them. We needed a steady flow of real content from stores nationwide.

I designed an internal creator programme and pitched it to the business. People joined through a survey. How it runs: creators bring ideas, I build the content plan, write the briefs, accept the work on deadline, and we publish on both their accounts and ours.

Motivation is status, not money: creators get seen, credited and featured. Being a creator became something people wanted inside the company — the system produces content without me now.

First national press coverage for the brand

03

Avito is Russia's largest classifieds platform — the local Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace combined — and its second-largest job search service. They invited MAAG into a New Year campaign alongside Sber, VkusVill, Samokat and Teremok (some of the biggest companies in Russia).

The creative from the brand side was mine: everyday retail jobs repackaged as holiday roles — "Become a stylist, a creator of fairytale looks," "Become a store manager, the leader of a magical team."

I aligned the concept with the team and leadership, ran communication with Avito and internal departments, prepared the seeding materials, and presented the results to stakeholders.

Careers website: a prototype in one working day

04

I realized the company needed its own site: to funnel traffic from the brand's own channels and stop depending on job boards, where we controlled nothing.

To skip the approval queue and avoid spending budget on a designer, I built a prototype on Tilda in one working day and showed it to the business. The idea got scaled up: a colleague and I worked out the visual language for a new version, and the project then moved to the design team, built on our demo.
